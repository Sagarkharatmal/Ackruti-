## TL;DR

A short explainer on **skiplagging** (a.k.a. hidden-city ticketing): booking a flight with a layover in the city you actually want, then *getting off at the layover* instead of flying the final leg — because the connecting itinerary can be cheaper than a direct ticket. It uses a concrete ATL→Orlando example ($250 direct vs. $130 if booked as ATL→Orlando→Dallas), introduces the booking site **Skiplagged**, references the airline lawsuit against it (on-screen "$94 million"), and closes with an advice card warning that **airlines may ban you** for doing it. Ends with a "follow for more" CTA.

- **CTA placement:** "follow for more" lands on the final, highest-retention frame.

## Accuracy caveats worth flagging
- The on-screen **"$94 million"** lawsuit figure should be sanity-checked before reusing — real reporting on the American Airlines v. Skiplagged case cites a substantially different damages number, so the figure in the reel may be inflated or misheard.
- Skiplagging is real but carries genuine risks the reel only glances at: airlines can cancel frequent-flyer accounts/miles, you can't check bags (they'd fly to the final city), and it breaks down on round-trips. Worth stating plainly if repurposing this content.

## Quick repurposing / improvement ideas
- Lead with the route diagram even earlier — it's the single clearest asset.
- Add an explicit on-screen line for the 3 risks (bags, account ban, round-trips) to make it more trustworthy and shareable.
- Verify and caption the exact lawsuit figure/date to avoid spreading a wrong number.
